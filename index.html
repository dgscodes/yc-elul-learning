The month of Elul

הַמֶּלֶךְ בַּשָּׂדֶה The King is in the field, and He is close enough to speak to.

The Alter Rebbe's mashal is famous for a reason. All year the King sits in His palace, behind gates and protocol. In Elul He walks out into the field, and anyone, in work clothes, mid-harvest, unprepared, can come over and be received with a smile.

Which is exactly why Elul learning carries what it carries. Torah learned in these twenty-nine days is learned while the gates are open. When that learning is dedicated in someone's name, the merit is credited to them: an aliyah for a neshama, a zechut for a refuah, a foundation under a simcha.

Our talmidim are in the Beit Midrash through Elul: morning seder from Monday to Friday, and night seder from Monday to Thursday. Every real learning slot can carry a name.

Why it works

Not a gesture. A mechanism.

Chazal are specific about what learning in someone's name does. Three sources worth sitting with.

כָּל תַּלְמִיד חָכָם שֶׁאוֹמְרִים דְּבַר שְׁמוּעָה מִפִּיו: שִׂפְתוֹתָיו דּוֹבְבוֹת בַּקֶּבֶר

"When a teaching is said in the name of one who has passed, his lips move with us in the grave."

Yevamot 97a

Not metaphor. Mechanism. The learning doesn't merely honour them; it includes them. They are still, in the only way that matters, in the Beit Midrash.

בְּרָא מְזַכֶּה אַבָּא

"A child brings merit to a parent."

Sanhedrin 104a

The merit of Torah moves upward through relationship. A sponsorship extends that chain: your name, your intention, their aliyah, carried by boys who will never meet them.

וְתַלְמוּד תּוֹרָה כְּנֶגֶד כֻּלָּם

"And the study of Torah is equal to them all."

Mishnah Peah 1:1

Of everything you could give in someone's name, the Mishnah puts this one on its own line. It is the merit that keeps producing after the giving stops.

Where it goes

Your sponsorship does two jobs at once.

It carries a name and funds the thing that carries it. Every rand goes into the learning programme and the Kfar trip that grows out of it.

01

Keeps the sedarim running

Seforim, mekorot sheets, refreshments, and the practical costs of optional out-of-hours learning before and after school.

02

Sends boys to Kfar

Subsidises the trip that connects our talmidim to their eternal homeland, for many, the first time they daven at the Kotel.

03

Exposes them to real yeshiva learning

Chavruta, iyun, a rebbe who knows their name. The kind of learning you can only meet in person.

04

Learns in yeshivot in Israel

The programme brings boys into real yeshiva learning in Israel: a serious environment, serious expectations, and Torah learned in the place it comes alive.

Questions

Before you sponsor.

Do the boys actually know whose name they're learning for?
Yes. Printed cards go on the shtenders before the seder starts, and the dedication is announced. It isn't a line in a newsletter. It's in front of them while they learn.
What if I don't know the Hebrew name?
Give us the English and whatever you have. If it's for a refuah, the custom is the person's name and their mother's name; for an iluy neshama it's the name and the father's. If you're unsure, send what you know and we'll help you get it right.
Can I sponsor a specific date, a yahrzeit or birthday?
That's exactly what the board is for. Pick the day, and if the seder is already taken we'll find you the closest one and tell you before anything is processed.
Can I sponsor anonymously?
Of course. The dedication goes up; your name doesn't have to. Just say so on the form.
Is there a minimum?
No. The amounts listed are what a seder, a day and a week cost us to run well. Anything given in a name is meaningful, and every sponsor gets the same card and the same announcement.
What happens after I submit the form?
You'll get a confirmation with the exact date and seder you've been allocated, and a copy of your dedication card. If anything needs fixing, we fix it before it's printed.
